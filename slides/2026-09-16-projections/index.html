function vander(x, k=nothing)
    if isnothing(k)
        k = length(x)
    end
    m = length(x)
    V = ones(m, k)
    for j in 2:k
        V[:, j] = V[:, j-1] .* x
    end
    V
end

Matrices as linear transformations¶

Linear algebra is the study of linear transformations on vectors, which represent points in a finite dimensional space. The matrix-vector product y=Axy = A xy=Ax is a linear combination of the columns of AAA. The familiar definition,

yi=∑jAi,jxjy_i = \sum_j A_{i,j} x_jyi​=j∑​Ai,j​xj​
(1)

can also be viewed as

y=[A:,1∣A:,2∣⋯][x1x2⋮]=[A:,1]x1+[A:,2]x2+⋯ .y = \Bigg[ A_{:,1} \Bigg| A_{:,2} \Bigg| \dotsm \Bigg] \begin{bmatrix} x_1 \\ x_2 \\ \vdots \end{bmatrix} = \Bigg[ A_{:,1} \Bigg] x_1 + \Bigg[ A_{:,2} \Bigg] x_2 + \dotsb .y=[A:,1​∣∣​A:,2​∣∣​⋯]⎣⎡​x1​x2​⋮​⎦⎤​=[A:,1​]x1​+[A:,2​]x2​+⋯.
(2)

Some common terminology¶

  • The range of AAA is the space spanned by its columns. This definition coincides with the range of a function f(x)f(x)f(x) when f(x)=Axf(x) = A xf(x)=Ax.

  • The (right) nullspace of AAA is the space of vectors xxx such that Ax=0A x = 0Ax=0.

  • The rank of AAA is the dimension of its range.

  • A matrix has full rank if the nullspace of either AAA or ATA^TAT is empty (only the 0 vector). Equivalently, if all the columns of AAA (or ATA^TAT) are linearly independent.

  • A nonsingular (or invertible) matrix is a square matrix of full rank. We call the inverse A−1A^{-1}A−1 and it satisfies A−1A=AA−1=IA^{-1} A = A A^{-1} = IA−1A=AA−1=I.

What is an inverse?¶

When we write x=A−1yx = A^{-1} yx=A−1y, we mean that xxx is the unique vector such that Ax=yA x = yAx=y. (It is rare that we explicitly compute a matrix A−1A^{-1}A−1, though it’s not as “bad” as people may have told you.) A vector yyy is equivalent to ∑ieiyi\sum_i \mathbf e_i y_i∑i​ei​yi​ where ei\mathbf e_iei​ are columns of the identity. Meanwhile, x=A−1yx = A^{-1} yx=A−1y means that we are expressing that same vector yyy in the basis of the columns of AAA, i.e., ∑iA:,ixi\sum_i A_{:,i} x_i∑i​A:,i​xi​.

Inner products and orthogonality¶

The inner product

xTy=∑ixiyix^T y = \sum_i x_i y_ixTy=i∑​xi​yi​
(3)

of vectors (or columns of a matrix) tell us about their magnitude and about the angle. The norm is induced by the inner product,

∥x∥=xTx\lVert x \rVert = \sqrt{x^T x}∥x∥=xTx​
(4)

and the angle θ\thetaθ is defined by

cos⁡θ=xTy∥x∥ ∥y∥.\cos \theta = \frac{x^T y}{\lVert x \rVert \, \lVert y \rVert} .cosθ=∥x∥∥y∥xTy​.
(5)

Inner products are bilinear, which means that they satisfy some convenient algebraic properties

(x+y)Tz=xTz+yTzxT(y+z)=xTy+xTz(αx)T(βy)=αβxTy.\begin{split} (x + y)^T z &= x^T z + y^T z \\ x^T (y + z) &= x^T y + x^T z \\ (\alpha x)^T (\beta y) &= \alpha \beta x^T y \\ \end{split} .(x+y)TzxT(y+z)(αx)T(βy)​=xTz+yTz=xTy+xTz=αβxTy​.
(6)

Geometry of Linear Algebra¶

We’d like to develop intuition about transformations on vectors. We’ll start with a peanut-shaped cluster of points and see how they transform.

default(aspect_ratio=:equal)

function peanut()
    theta = LinRange(0, 2*pi, 50)
    r = 1 .+ .4*sin.(3*theta) + .6*sin.(2*theta)
    x = r .* cos.(theta)
    y = r .* sin.(theta)
    x, y
end

Givens Rotation¶

We can rotate the input using a 2×22\times 22×2 matrix, parametrized by θ\thetaθ.

function givens(theta)
    s = sin(theta)
    c = cos(theta)
    A = [c -s; s c]
    A
end
